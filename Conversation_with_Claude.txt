  The three items, done

  1. Docstring corrected — trajectory.py claimed FALCON reparameterises its knots for velocity feasibility. It has the machinery (reallocateTime) and never calls it; the knots are uniform in
  practice and feasibility is only a soft optimiser cost. The docstring now says so, with the flight evidence.

  2. Drag feedforward — drag_per_mps/drag_offset_mps2 in the tracker, computed from the planned velocity (measured would be positive feedback on speed). Defaults zero in core; the Pegasus
  mission and stub set the measured curve 0.176·v + 0.121 m/s² via a new _default_tracking().

  3. Attitude lead — attitude_lead_s: the feedforward acceleration and jerk are sampled 0.18 s ahead of the reference, so the aircraft is commanded the attitude the plan wants at the moment
  the airframe will actually reach it. Feedback stays at the reference — it corrects errors that exist, not errors that are predicted.

  Validated deterministically, and the data corrected me

  A live stub run couldn't validate this (FALCON's variance swamps it), so the new test_feedforward_flight.py flies the stub's AttitudeAircraft — which models exactly these two effects — A/B
  on a fixed corner route:

  ┌───────────────┬─────────────────┬─────────┐
