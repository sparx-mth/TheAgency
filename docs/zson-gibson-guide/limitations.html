Chapter 5 · Critical assessment

What is not working well?

Verified implementation issues, measured failure patterns, and a staged plan for applying the architecture to a genuinely broader ZSON problem.

5.1 · Existing flaws

Mechanism, evidence, and improvement

A demonstrated behavior is not always a demonstrated root cause. The labels below distinguish source-verified issues, direct probes, recorded outcomes and design inferences. None of the proposed changes was implemented during this documentation task.

1. Premature bounded termination Measured

Mechanism: a burst ends; its region is cooling/at its revisit cap; no other region is eligible; SELECT immediately enters DONE. The useful-partial exception applies specifically to action-budget expiry with sufficient gain, not every blockage/low-gain ending. A fixed 4 m scope can also finish while a large room remains partly unseen.

Why flawed: “no region presently eligible” is a scheduling/reachability outcome, not proof the task is impossible. It can permanently fail with most of the global budget left. Mifflinburg, Allensville and Pinesdale show early failures; Newfields shows the longer version.

Improve: distinguish cooldown, temporary map/planning failure, exhausted geometry and global-budget termination. Add bounded reason-specific re-observation/recovery and authorized scope expansion/re-anchoring. Do not fix early failure by making retries unbounded or driving through unknown cells.

2. The probability model is not yet a complete ZSON search model Verified

Mechanism: RPT multiplies independent miss probabilities; the oracle supplies normalized room weights scaled by mapped-presence mass. Those are not calibrated independent chances of detecting an acceptable instance during a finite search.

Why flawed: for one mutually exclusive target location, surviving masses 0.3 and 0.3 leaves 0.4, not the product 0.49. But Gibson can contain multiple acceptable instances, so switching blindly to a single-target formula is not universally correct either. Detection yield also depends on visibility, detector recall and time spent searching.

Improve: first define single instance, any category instance, multiple required instances or possible absence. Model success under bounded visual effort; then derive/test objective, heuristic and pruning together. Changing only the survival recurrence does not automatically preserve correctness guarantees.

3. “Expected time to find” omits search and turn costs Verified wiring

Both active room-matrix calls leave search service time and frontier blending at zero. RPT costs are distance divided by nominal forward speed, while actual room search, turns, collisions and verification consume many actions. An ordering optimal for transit can fail under the action budget.

Improve: estimate entry/turn/search cost and detection yield per bounded region visit; test budget-aware search objectives. Revalidate assumptions for heading/history-dependent costs. Report SPL for comparability but do not call it wall-time or action efficiency.

4. Room costs and executable paths disagree at diagonal corners Direct CPU reproduction

The room-cost graph connects finite diagonal cells without requiring orthogonal side cells to be passable. FALCON’s free-route graph rejects that corner cutting.

Minimal executed check:

In a 2 × 2 grid with only opposite diagonal cells free, the room-cost graph returns √2 cells. FALCON’s route distances report unreachable. This is a synthetic geometry probe, not a Gibson score.

Why flawed: the matrix may underestimate a detour or assert a physically impossible connection. Triangle inequality can still hold on the wrong graph; a mathematical guarantee does not repair physical-model mismatch.

Improve: share the no-corner-cut passability contract between costs and execution; add corner-touch/narrow-portal/swept-footprint regressions. Also bound matrix construction itself: the 12-room solver cap happens after all-room cost work.

5. Repeated detections are not robust target identity Failures recorded; cause mixed

Three frames and one sufficient viewpoint change can confirm a hypothesis. Correlated errors can satisfy this; nearby same-class instances can merge within 0.70 m, while changing labels create separate hypotheses. The map keeps averaged points, not competing 3D identity/label beliefs. Box depth can represent the wrong surface.

Ranchester, Woodbine and Wainscott stop with policy-confirmed targets but fail the evaluator. The records do not uniquely separate detector confusion, association/range, approach and annotation/scorer alignment.

Improve: label these failure causes independently; retain segmentation/spatial support and competing labels; require informative independent views; add visibility-conditioned negative evidence. ApexNav/SG-Nav mechanisms are relevant hypotheses, not guaranteed cures. Measure any VLM verification’s latency and correlated-error risk.

6. Room affinity is over-interpreted as calibrated probability Verified

Affinity anchors, shrinkage, size, decay and noisy-OR are handcrafted. Provisional/low-confidence room labels still enter the affinity prompt as selected types. Six alphabetically sorted unique classes can omit important context; area influences both the prompt and numerical weight. Credited room time can include turning/transit rather than inspection.

Improve: propagate room-type uncertainty, prioritize target-relevant evidence, calibrate search yield on development scenes and discount by visibility-qualified negative evidence. Compare against fixed-prior/no-LLM ablations before attributing improvements to language reasoning.

7. The named half-life is actually an e-folding time Direct CPU reproduction

The effort decay uses exp(−elapsed/scale). At 90 action-clock seconds in a 20 m² room with remaining frontiers, the factor is 0.367879, not 0.5. The effective half-life is about 62.4 seconds.

Why flawed: a researcher tuning a stated 90-second half-life gets different behavior. Improve: rename/document an e-folding scale, or use a logarithm-of-two factor for true half-life semantics. A behavior change requires new configuration identity and measurements; do not retroactively relabel results.

8. Room geometry can drift without the right evidence reset Verified structure

Partition revision tracks room-ID/door-ID sets, so mask shape can change without revision. Conversely, changed sets can reset stability broadly. Strict point-in-free-room-mask assignment can leave obstacle-surface objects unassigned. Labels, effort or visit history may therefore describe a changed region or omit useful objects.

Improve: version meaningful mask changes, transfer evidence by geometric overlap, assign objects with boundary/nearest-region uncertainty, and evaluate room segmentation/classification separately. FALCON overlap history improves budget continuity but is not a probabilistic room-identity model.

9. Completion checks may repeatedly read the same evidence Verified cadence interaction

The default supervisor asks for three zero-frontier updates while room facts normally refresh every ten observations. Three calls can read the same graph measurement. Frontier-count stagnation also does not mean zero useful area or no target-bearing views.

Improve: debounce on fresh graph revisions and combine count with visibility, gain and reachability. Separate observed geometric completion from exhaustive target inspection.

10. Stable commitment can keep stale choices Design trade-off

Ignoring small probability changes during transit prevents oscillation. Yet the default may keep an order suffix despite major probability drift, while FALCON re-solves after each region and executes only the first room. These are different approximations to receding-horizon search.

Improve: retain short-term commitment but reconsider meaningful expected improvement at safe boundaries. Record proposals versus executed room changes and test hysteresis. Calling RPT every frame is not a principled solution.

11. Conservative geometry and recovery can deadlock Blockage measured

Unknown/inflation can eliminate outgoing moves. FALCON adds a stricter action veto; recovery rotates rather than using a new reverse primitive. A failed forward is stamped ahead as obstacle evidence, but no movement alone does not localize an obstacle. Persistent map mistakes can trigger terminal failure.

Improve: separate contact evidence from confident obstacle geometry; use bounded alternative views and retained safe escape paths; test narrow-gap action-lattice feasibility. Never delete real obstacles just to make a desired path possible. Separate the safety-layer ablation from the explorer ablation.

12. Coverage and target search are different objectives Objective-based inference

FALCON CP/SOP organizes coverage, while ObjectNav rewards detecting a target before a limit. Local FALCON is geometric; semantics enter at boundaries, not every viewpoint. A view revealing floor area may offer little target-detection value.

Improve: test target-conditioned information gain and adaptive geometric/semantic exploration. Higher observed m²/action is insufficient if reliability falls.

13. Error handling and counters differ across variants Verified control flow

Frontier propagates room-LLM internal failures; FALCON records them and terminates. Model/service failures can therefore enter aggregate outcomes differently. Final graph counters reset on floor changes, and accepted affinity rounds are not total requests.

Improve: standardize failure taxonomy and experiment policy, record immutable episode-wide call counts/retries/latencies, and distinguish navigation failure from unavailable infrastructure. The campaign’s zero agent errors alone is not a universal accounting guarantee.

14. The benchmark does not establish deployment robustness Known scope

Ideal depth/pose, static reconstructions, no dynamics, six categories and a planar start-floor scorer define a controlled experiment. Success dilation need not prove final visibility. Habitat versions differ. OpenAI-compatible requests also do not necessarily receive every seed/token option used in the Ollama path, despite a nominal shared configuration.

Improve: preserve the reference profile and name separate sensor-noise, multi-floor, action and embodiment profiles. Record actual request settings. Never pool robustness results into the published Gibson table.

15. Sampling does not support a broad superiority claim Measured limit

The latest batch has one start in each of 15 training buildings and no TV episode. Earlier validation starts were inspected. No full current-method 1,000-episode result was established. Freezing ensures reproducibility, not unseen data.

Improve: broaden training-development coverage across categories, distances and headings, predeclare ablations, then freeze and evaluate the entire published validation set with all failures/caveats. Report intervals and per-scene/category behavior.

5.2 · Applying the architecture

What transfers to the new ZSON problem?

Reuse the separation of public observations, search memory, navigation commands, legal actions and independent GT evaluation. Define what changes instead of assuming a new language prompt creates a new benchmark.

Task questionCurrent Gibson answerWhat a broader task needs
What object counts?Any acceptable annotated instance of six exact categories.Category, attribute, relation, named-instance or compositional grounding and matching GT.
Can it be absent?Loader requires a nonempty category map.Absent-target episodes and a scored abstention/search-completeness rule.
What proves success?Zero final FMM distance in start-floor field.Explicit distance, visibility, identity, floor, STOP and orientation requirements.
Which sensors?Ideal RGB-D and perfect pose.Declare RGB-only, estimated pose or noisy depth as distinct profiles.
Which actions?Forward, yaw turns, STOP.Specify costs and adapters if adding LOOK, continuous control, stairs or manipulation.
How is tuning separated?No task-specific training, but known validation development exposure.Freeze tuning/evaluation scenes, models, prompts and allowed adaptation.

Three levels of extension

  1. Same categories, new starts: reuse existing mapping/aliases/actions/scoring; develop on generated training starts and retain original validation.
  2. New categories/language: extend detector vocabulary and grounding contract and obtain corresponding category/instance annotations. The existing GT planes cannot score “a red chair beside a desk” or an absent fire extinguisher.
  3. New environments/sensing: adapt place/room representation, perception and action interfaces. A fixed room-type list plus floor-reset map is not full open-world, multi-floor navigation.

Recommended staged plan

  1. Repair consistency first: no-corner-cut costs, fresh-evidence debouncing and error accounting, with small regression scenarios.
  2. Fix premature termination: distinguish temporary ineligibility from task failure; retain useful partial search without removing budget/safety guarantees.
  3. Audit target failure causes: recognition, association, approach/range and GT alignment need separate labels.
  4. Define the probability model: category/multi-instance/absence semantics and detection under finite search effort must agree with routing.
  5. Add one semantic mechanism at a time: OSG connectors/schema, SG-Nav contextual re-perception, ApexNav negative multi-label fusion/adaptive exploration.
  6. Run matched training-development ablations: hold starts, detector, LLM and scoring fixed, then test the integrated hierarchy.
  7. Freeze for complete validation: retain exposure disclosures; report new protocols separately.

Do not pass GT rooms/goals into observations to “help” planning, treat an LLM negative as proof of absence, give free panoramas, silently change success radius, tune repeatedly on full validation, or transfer a numerical RPT guarantee to the entire mission.

Proposed plan, not implemented changes. Grounded in the preceding audit, C08: interfaces, C21: protocol, and P1/P2/P3.

5.3 · Testing and reproducibility

What has actually been validated?

1,892 CPU-only tests passed during this audit.

ObjectNav runtime/public contracts/converter, exploration, RPT*, object mapping, topology and the shared harness: 16.89 seconds, 19 warnings. No new simulator episode, rendering workload, model-weight download or training was performed.

Warnings concerned deprecated morphology operations and the local Matplotlib 3D projection setup, not failed 2D/scoring tests. The direct diagonal/decay probes were separate checks. A green suite does not certify the semantic model or benchmark reliability.

Inspecting a saved run without reading code

Use the workstation’s optional paired recording gallery. The included guide images remain available even if that machine-local directory is absent.

  • Campaign manifest/locks: exact buildings, starts, model/settings/source identity and job order fixed before execution.
  • Per-run configuration: actual explorer, detector checkpoint, LLM, runtime, protocol and fingerprints.
  • Episode record/summary: final metrics, success versus STOP, actions/path/errors and policy diagnostics.
  • Per-step decisions/trajectory: emitted symbol, observed pose, current state, predictions and reasoning. Policy route distance is not GT DTG.
  • Evaluator sidecar: GT distances, native collisions and first success entry, never injected into policy observations.
  • Coverage/statistics: proxy gain over actions, intervals and paired tests; beware unequal termination horizons.
  • Video validation: all 30 H.264 recordings decode with actions+1 frames including terminal observation.

Publication-readiness checklist

  1. Declare release, complete episode list and role.
  2. Freeze source, models, prompts, settings, seed and action/sensor geometry.
  3. Validate frame conversions, GT isolation, actual motion and score agreement.
  4. Keep all outcomes and use a consistent error taxonomy.
  5. Report SR/SPL together, DTG, action/wall cost, intervals and category/scene breakdowns.
  6. Disclose validation exposure, category imbalance, simulator mismatch and privileged sensors.
  7. Keep published validation, training development and new robustness protocols separate.
