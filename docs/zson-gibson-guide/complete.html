1.1 · Orientation

What is “our algorithm”?

The active method is a training-free, metric, hierarchical object-search system. It builds an observed map from RGB-D and perfect simulator pose, detects objects, infers rooms, uses a language model to judge room relevance, and uses RPT* to order room visits. A local explorer and geometric path follower turn those decisions into discrete simulator actions.

ZSON means zero-shot object navigation: given a target category, such as toilet, search an unfamiliar environment without navigation-specific training for this task. It does not mean that the detector or LLM was never trained, that the target category was unseen in pretraining, or that the implemented evaluator supports arbitrary language. The papers also mention an earlier method literally named ZSON; that named baseline is not this implementation.

Default: frontier

Recorded name: sparx-rpt-llm-host-sweep. Periodic room reasoning, a SELECT → TRANSIT → SEARCH room supervisor, RPT room ordering, largest-frontier-cluster selection inside a room, and weighted-cost A* routes.

Optional: bounded FALCON

Recorded name: sparx-rpt-llm-falcon-planar. Bounded local coverage exploration → room reasoning → RPT selection → A* transit → another local burst, with target-verification and recovery interrupts.

These are different loops, not one interchangeable planner.

The FALCON variant changes local exploration, reasoning cadence, region budgets and the final action-safety layer. A comparison between them measures an integrated hierarchy change, not an isolated frontier-selector ablation. Frontier remains the default because current development evidence favors its reliability.

The essential distinctions

  1. Gibson is scene data; Habitat-Sim is the simulator. Episodes and a scorer define the benchmark.
  2. Ideal depth and pose are allowed inputs; hidden semantic goal maps are not. “Observed-only” does not mean “no simulator-derived ground truth.”
  3. RPT* orders destinations; A* finds geometric paths. RPT* is not the motion-sampling algorithm RRT*.
  4. Room type, affinity, probability and detector confidence differ. None is automatically calibrated GT.
  5. Target confirmation, STOP and success differ. Our Gibson scorer judges final position, does not require STOP at the step limit, and does not end the episode at first success-region entry.

Scope and evidence labels

This guide covers the actual ObjectNav benchmark path, mapping/perception/room reasoning, route and local planners, actions, evaluation, recording and tests. It excludes SPHERA control, ROS flight-control chains, autopilots and unrelated deployment infrastructure. Some reusable source comments say “aircraft”; here their abstractions control a kinematic ground agent.

Implemented means traced in the current source. Measured means read from saved records or an explicitly described CPU probe. Proposed/inference means an interpretation or future change, not a feature claimed to run today. Published numbers are attributed to their source paper.

1.2 · Simulation

Gibson, Habitat and the simulated robot

Gibson contains reconstructions of real indoor buildings. A textured 3D mesh describes surfaces and their appearance. Habitat-Sim places a virtual agent/camera in that geometry, renders an image from its current viewpoint, and constrains movement with a navigation mesh. The input is rendered from a 3D reconstruction, not a prerecorded movie played to the policy.

LayerWhat it suppliesImportant boundary

Scene .navmeshNative navigable surfaces/connectivity.Used for simulator movement; no unseen oracle route is passed to the policy.

The current agent is synchronous, kinematic and upright, with physics disabled. Its nominal radius is 0.18 m. Colocated RGB/depth cameras sit 0.88 m above the base. There are no motor dynamics, acceleration model, battery, grasping, articulated-door interaction or flight controller in this benchmark.

The bridge loads the released navmesh rather than recomputing it. Reset checks that the simulator preserves the published start; difficult starts are not silently snapped to an easier location. Sliding is enabled: a forward attempt may move fully, partly, sideways along an obstacle, or hardly at all. Actual displacement—not requested displacement—determines path length.

Texture seams, incomplete surfaces and ambiguous furniture come from the reconstruction. Ideal depth is accurate relative to that mesh, not necessarily the original real building. Perfect geometric depth does not solve recognition or annotation errors.

Why motion looks jumpy

A 30° discrete turn produces a new view without giving the agent intermediate frames. Saved videos normally play six decisions per second, not measured real time. Optional smooth replay interpolates views after evaluation; those frames never enter the policy or scorer. Smoother video is not better sensing.

Runtime caveat.

The reference SemExp setup used Habitat-Sim 0.1.5; local recorded runs use 0.2.4. The CLI requires acknowledgment of the mismatch. This is an explicitly documented SemExp-derived port, not demonstrated bit-exact OSG Navigator reproduction.

Depth conventions

Usable depth lies between 0.5 and 5.0 m. Returns at/below the near clip, including zero/hole pixels, become NaN: unusable measurement. At/beyond the far clip they become positive infinity: clipped/no surface within range. Back-projection discards invalid/clipped samples. It must not invent a wall at the far clipping plane. Depth is optical-axis distance, not the full radial length of each viewing ray.

Coordinate conventions

Habitat uses +Y up and local −Z forward. The public right-handed Z-up coordinates are: x = −Habitat Z; y = −Habitat X; z = Habitat Y. Yaw is counterclockwise about public +Z. Public z is the base/floor elevation; camera height is added separately. Positive public camera pitch looks down. The bridge and shared projection helpers perform these conversions once.

What is not automatically supplied?

No perfect room boundaries/names, detector boxes, semantic camera pixels, instance tracks, language descriptions or open-vocabulary GT reach this policy. The six category planes cannot evaluate an arbitrary new phrase merely because an LLM understands the words.

Boundary, not security sandbox.

1.4 · Simulator interaction

What does one action mean?

An action is one discrete instruction, not an internal path, a velocity vector, an LLM sentence or a batch of images. The policy proposes a navigation command; the converter emits one legal symbol; Habitat executes it; then the agent decides from the new observation.

Legal actionInternal IDNominal effectAccounting
MOVE_FORWARD1Attempt 0.25 m in the current heading; collision/sliding may change actual movement.One action even if blocked; add measured horizontal distance.
TURN_LEFT2Yaw +30° in place.One action, normally no path distance.
TURN_RIGHT3Yaw −30° in place.One action, normally no path distance.
STOP0End the episode and preserve its final view.One action, no movement; may represent success or failure.

The shared framework defines LOOK_UP/DOWN, but our Gibson profile does not enable them. It has no reverse, strafe, teleport, open-door, pick-up, continuous-velocity or free-panorama action. Reset can place the agent at the fixed start only. Extra simulator/UI capabilities do not become legal benchmark actions.

Illustration—not a recorded trial.

At (0,0), heading 0°, TURN_LEFT followed by an unobstructed MOVE_FORWARD ends near (0.217, 0.125) m at 30°. Two actions, 0.25 m of travel. If forward is blocked, the action is still spent and the next decision uses the actual pose.

The path-to-action ladder

  1. Emit STOP if explicitly requested.
  2. Handle requested camera tilt only if the profile allows it; Gibson does not.
  3. Project the robot onto the current path leg, retaining forward-only progress. Do not jump to a later return leg simply because it is close.
  4. Normally aim 0.5 m ahead. If heading error exceeds 15°, turn toward the aim point.
  5. Otherwise move forward when it makes useful progress. Near the path’s end, apply arrival tolerance and any requested final heading.
  6. If no action remains, the headless agent emits an idle TURN_LEFT. No path is not automatically STOP.
  7. FALCON checks the actual forward proposal against observed footprint-safe cells/scope and can replace it with a charged left turn/recovery.

The default endpoint tolerance is 0.25 m. The converter also respects a quantization-derived reach floor of about 0.129 m, but the larger configured tolerance governs here. Arrival is based on actual path progress, including snapped endpoints; it is not merely distance to the original requested centroid and is not GT success.

Generating training-development episodes

  1. Select 15 training names by seeded deterministic ordering before running either policy.
  2. Select an available floor/category with recorded seeds.
  3. Sample a navigable point near that floor, with 0.5 m floor tolerance, a GT free semantic cell, and reference start distance strictly between 1.5 and 100 m.

Known validation exposure must remain disclosed.

Further systematic tuning should use training scenes, and future full-validation results must retain the disclosure.

Zero-shot does not rule out tuning leakage: prompts, thresholds, room rules and budgets can be tuned without updating model weights. The papers do not establish a complete Gibson parameter-selection history or absence of foundation-model pretraining overlap. Nor is there evidence to accuse their authors of overfitting. 2.1 · End-to-end execution

From starting the benchmark to saving a score

  1. Prepare →Verify data, services, source and configuration.
  2. Reset →Install start; clear episode memory.
  3. Observe →Receive posed RGB-D.
  4. Decide →Map, detect, reason and plan as needed.
  5. Act →Execute exactly one legal symbol.
  6. JudgeAt termination, score and record.

Before the first movement

  1. Resolve the experiment. Select explorer, role, exact episode list, seed and output. An explicit explorer flag cannot disagree with the configuration file. The separate STOP-only agent is a plumbing diagnostic, not the search method.
  2. Validate data. Check scene pairs, category/floor consistency, finite positions, unit quaternions and valid GT lookups. Full validation requires all 1,000 rows, including the reference finite-sentinel case; do not remove inconvenient published starts.
  3. Validate models. Check actual detector backend/checkpoint/configuration, vocabulary order and LLM identity. A live HTTP port is not enough. Refuse a detector emission threshold that hides low-confidence door proposals.
  4. Record provenance. Save source/data fingerprints, runtime, full method settings, models, seed and sensor/action protocol. Credentials stay out of result artifacts.
  5. Check resources. Habitat owns rendering; the recorded detector and LLM are CPU services. No ROS/flight stack is involved.
  6. Freeze and resume correctly. Full-validation freeze/run locks configuration before execution. A resumed experiment must match source, data and configuration, and skips only complete recorded episodes.

Reset is part of the method

The evaluator prepares the private field and reference distance. The simulator loads the building/navmesh and exact start. The headless agent receives only public task/sensor/action information, maps the target category to exact aliases, and initializes its converter.

The policy resets its map, landmarks, doors, scene graph, labels, target support, room supervisor, routes and telemetry. RPT gets a reproducible episode-dependent fallback random generator. FALCON additionally initializes a local planner, burst ledger, region history and route bank. Model weights/configuration can persist, but a previous episode’s learned map is not reused. Evaluator field caching does not become policy memory.

Every observation-to-action iteration

  1. Validate the observation. Check category, camera and increasing step index. If the last forward action moved less than 0.01 m, notify the policy before planning. The policy invalidates the route and adds local blockage evidence inferred from pose—not hidden collision geometry.
  2. Integrate depth. Update the body-height map and observed-area telemetry; reset incompatible floor state if elevation changes substantially. Credit the current inferred room with action-clock time. Movement above 0.05 m clears the sustained-blockage clock.
  3. Run perception. Detect objects from RGB, confirm doors, remove duplicates, project objects with depth and update target evidence. This continues during exploration, transit, recovery and target approach.
  4. Apply target and graph rules. A fresh supported target within 0.75 m can produce STOP. Otherwise refresh geometry when its period or door revision requires it. Frontier enables reasoning there; FALCON defers queries to a reasoning boundary.
  5. Choose a navigation command. The active target/room/local state chooses a path, final yaw, hold or STOP. Reuse safe committed routes where possible.
  6. Convert, guard and charge. Choose one discrete action. FALCON may replace an unsafe forward with a legal turn. Charge the emitted action once to the global and any active local/interrupt budgets.
  7. Execute, inspect and repeat. Habitat returns the next observation. The harness validates realized motion and accounting. STOP or the step limit ends the loop; measure independently and log.

A zero-motion state transition is computation, not a simulator action. A detector or LLM taking several seconds does not provide extra free views. A terminal observation can be recorded without asking the policy for another action.

2.2 · Observed geometry

How the map is built

The default policy map is 80 × 80 m at 0.1 m/cell: 800 × 800 cells centered on the first observed position. It starts unknown except for direct current-footprint evidence. It is not the evaluator’s 5 cm GT floor grid.

  1. Back-project valid depth. Camera intrinsics map pixels to optical-frame 3D points; the shared transform puts them in world coordinates. Stride 8 samples roughly an 80 × 60 lattice before clipping/validity filtering.
  2. Integrate a ground-robot obstacle slab. Keep obstacle evidence between floor anchor +0.15 m and body height +0.05 m, which is +0.93 m under the 0.88 m default. The planar map records whether relevant vertical columns obstruct the body.
  3. Update log odds. Depth rays/endpoints provide free/occupied evidence through the existing depth integrator. Repeated measurements accumulate; this is not a new perfect map each frame.
  4. Add visible floor evidence conservatively. Points within 0.10 m of the floor anchor make only their own cells freer. They do not carve rays through/under furniture; existing occupied columns take precedence.
  5. Encode the planner states. Probabilities ≤45 become free, ≥65 occupied; unobserved/intermediate values remain unknown. Public labels are free 0, occupied 100, unknown −1.
  6. Include measured footprint. The body’s current footprint is direct embodiment evidence. It does not authorize clearing a future goal. Occupied cells are retained, except the measured base cell is explicitly forced reachable.

Unknown blocks executable motion. Near the fixed map boundary, the method raises rather than querying unseen scene bounds. A vertical change above 0.6 m resets map and incompatible room/object/route geometry. Ordinary map updates do not reset everything. This protects against overlaying floors but does not implement persistent multi-floor search.

2.3 · Semantic evidence

Objects, doors and rooms are inferred

Object proposals and target vocabulary

The completed default detector is pretrained YOLO-World X-v2 on a dedicated service. Pretrained LLMDet Swin-L is selectable, but the latest matched campaign used X-v2 for both explorers. Actual service identity controls the result; an older saved demo can retain an earlier explicit checkpoint.

The client checks health/identity, encodes the current RGB through the shared JPEG/BGR service contract and verifies returned image dimensions, vocabulary, confidence values and boxes. Proposals are emitted down to 0.05 for doors; normal navigation objects need 0.35. The room LLM does not classify the raw camera image in this policy.

  1. Deduplicate. Canonicalize couch/sofa and TV/television. Suppress same-class boxes at overlap ≥0.5 IoU, and near-identical cross-label boxes at overlap ≥0.9.
  2. Estimate depth. Use the central half-width/half-height of the box; require 20 valid pixels and take their 30th percentile. This reduces background contamination but is still a bounding-box approximation.
  3. Project the hypothesis. Use box-center ray and robust depth to obtain a world point. Store horizontal x/y, not a complete object mesh.
  4. Associate over time. Match the nearest same-class landmark within 0.70 m; otherwise allocate an ID. Update its running-average position at most once per frame. Two observations make an ordinary landmark available to the scene graph.
  5. Keep target support separate. A candidate can guide approach before confirmation; target STOP uses the stricter multi-view/freshness gate in the execution chapter.

Only exact accepted aliases satisfy an episode. Sink, refrigerator, desk and other context classes can influence room reasoning but cannot satisfy a toilet goal. Detector confidence, landmark observation count and target-confirmation support are different quantities.

Doors require geometry and multiple views

Door, doorway, open doorway and door-frame candidates use narrow jamb/lintel depth bands because the center of an open door often measures the far wall beyond it. At least two valid bands are required. The estimator checks plausible width 0.35–1.5 m, top/bottom geometry and overlapping alternative explanations such as a confident cabinet.

Confirmation needs at least three observations, deduplication within 0.60 m, and at least 0.20 m translation or 20° yaw separation. New confirmed IDs change the door revision and can trigger early room refresh. These are approximate observed portals, not GT architectural entrances.

Geometric rooms come before room names

A clearance-based watershed segments the observed free-space mask around bottlenecks; confirmed doors assist cuts. Defaults: 1.0 m minimum room separation, 0.3 m minimum clearance, 50-cell minimum region, 0.75 m door-cut setting and 0.2 m merge dynamics. Fifty 10 cm cells represent 0.5 m², not a guarantee of an architectural room.

A registry matches regions by overlap, using IoU threshold 0.15, and maintains masks, centroids and areas. Frontier clusters and door adjacency are measured. Object landmarks are assigned by their projected point falling inside the room mask. Only afterward can the LLM label a room kitchen, bathroom, etc.

A room is an evolving inferred region.

A first view can produce no room, one provisional region or several fragments. Observations split/merge them. Their IDs are not Gibson GT IDs. Concave-room centroids can lie inside walls, and projected object points on occupied surfaces may not fall in free-space room masks.

The current graph is substantially simpler than the papers’ richer scene graphs: rooms, confirmed-door links, object-category lists, area, frontier count and accumulated room time. The target-affinity prompt receives up to six sorted unique class names per room, not a complete object-relation graph, image or unseen building.

2.4 · The language model

Two LLM jobs, not one probability oracle

A. Classify a room

Input: counted confirmed object classes from one region.

Output: one allowed room type, self-reported confidence and a short rationale.

What kind of room does this evidence suggest?

B. Score target affinity

Input: target plus all current room IDs, types, size and compact object evidence.

Output: an affinity and explanation per room.

How typical is this target in a room like that?

The default is Qwen2.5 3B Instruct, temperature 0, seed 0, 768 response-token limit, 30 s request timeout. Actual environment-resolved backend/model/settings are recorded; the 15-building campaign used this model on CPU. Semantic judgments come from the LLM, arithmetic and motion choices from code.

Room classification gates and refresh

  1. Require at least three confirmed unique landmarks, at least two canonical classes, and evidence over at least two distinct graph-update indices. Duplicate calls on the same observation do not count as new evidence.
  2. Before that gate, label the region unknown without a classification call.
  3. At a query-enabled reasoning opportunity, query when the counted class signature changed or 50 steps elapsed since that room’s last query. Explicit refresh bypasses the core classifier’s signature cache.
  4. Otherwise retain its answer. The 50-step rule is checked only at a reasoning opportunity, not by an independent timer.
  5. Mark the label provisional if unknown, confidence <0.65, or fewer than two classification calls agree. That provisional flag is diagnostic; the affinity prompt still receives the selected label rather than a room-type distribution.
  6. On a detected partition change, clear stability/signature/agreement bookkeeping. The current partition revision checks room-ID and confirmed-door-ID sets, not every mask-shape change.

The label list includes household and some institutional room types, but it is fixed. This is not automatically generated OSG schema reasoning over arbitrary stores, airports or buildings.

Affinity-to-probability transformation

The LLM is asked for independent semantic 0–100 affinity scores, not a distribution summing to 100. Unknown means unidentified, not empty. The prompt hides elapsed search time and frontier counts so the LLM does not duplicate the numerical effort penalty.

  1. Clamp and soften: clamp to 3–90; retain 70% of each clamped score plus 30% of the mean. Omitted rooms receive the mean, not zero.
  2. Modest size weighting: multiply by (area / 20 m²)0.25, bounded to 0.6–1.6.
  3. Effort weighting: apply exponential decay with a 90-second base scale, slowed for rooms larger than 20 m². With remaining frontiers, do not discount below 0.25. With no frontier clusters, use 0.15 times the decay.
  4. Relative ranking: normalize the weighted scores over mapped rooms to sum to one.
  5. Mapped presence mass: use a heuristic noisy-OR of softened affinity/effort, with per-room scale 0.5. The residual represents target mass elsewhere.
  6. RPT input: multiply each relative weight by that mapped-presence mass. Do not renormalize away the residual when filtering/capping.
Relative room weight ∝ softened affinity × size factor × effort factor.
RPT room value = relative weight × estimated presence in mapped rooms.

The default supervisor filters by relative weight. The routing adapter reads the scaled probabilities from the cost instance, not the candidates’ renormalized weights. FALCON builds candidates from reachable eligible region goals and those scaled values. This distinction prevents one surviving room from becoming naive certainty.

These are uncalibrated heuristics.

There is no learned calibration, explicit detection-recall model or Bayesian negative-observation update. Credited time is not complete visual coverage. “Elsewhere” is not an explicit RPT vertex: reserving mass does not itself generate an action into unseen space.

Model failures and costs

Transport calls allow two attempts with a short backoff; the oracle allows one additional schema-repair query. One accepted affinity round can therefore involve several requests. Unusable oracle output is not silently accepted as uniform reasoning by the benchmark.

Frontier propagates room-reasoning failure as an internal error that aborts the run. FALCON records the error in hierarchy diagnostics and transitions to DONE. This different treatment matters for fair error accounting; neither means “the target is absent.” The parameter called effort half-life actually implements an e-folding scale, as verified and explained in the flaws chapter.

2.5 · Global ordering

RPT*, iteration by iteration

RPT* asks which order of probabilistic destinations reduces expected travel before a target is found. It is a search over room orders, not a continuous robot-motion planner.

Build its numerical problem

  1. Select representatives. The default cost builder starts with centroids and snaps blocked centers to passable cells. FALCON first chooses a reachable goal inside each eligible room, preferring an interior entry.
  2. Add the robot depot. Its current pose becomes a separate probability-zero vertex. Starting in a room does not consume that room as already searched.
  3. Construct costs. Build a physical-radius passability graph and compute Dijkstra distances from representative cells. Edge lengths use geometric steps, not A* clearance penalties.
  4. Withhold disconnected rooms. RPT requires a complete finite matrix. They can return when observation connects the map; do not assign fake huge finite distances.
  5. Convert to proxy time. The benchmark uses 0.25 m per action-clock second: a metre becomes four nominal forward seconds. Both active callers leave optional search service time and frontier blending at zero.
  6. Cap the solve at 12 rooms. Reserve one nearest-room slot, then fill by probability with distance tie-breaks. Record skipped/capped IDs. The all-room cost build precedes this cap.
  7. Translate indices correctly. The matrix builder appends the depot; the routing package prepends its synthetic start. Reorder rows/columns and translate the solution back to room IDs.

These costs are estimates. Actual behavior also spends search, turning, failed motion and verification actions. Default transit may still command a raw centroid rather than the matrix’s snapped representative; FALCON’s reachable inside-room goal reduces that mismatch.

Why expected cost depends on order

A shortest tour pays every edge in full. A target-search route only pays a late edge if nothing was found earlier. RPT carries q, the modeled probability of still searching.

Edge contribution = current q × travel cost.
After visiting a room with value p, update q to q × (1 − p).

This is the implemented independent probabilistic-terminal model, not automatically the right model for one mutually exclusive target. Optimality applies to this numerical problem, not the entire partially observed navigation mission.

Inside one solve

  1. Validate costs/probabilities and metric assumptions required for pruning. Each p must be below 1.
  2. Initialize at the synthetic depot, expected cost zero, survival one, depot already marked visited.
  3. Precompute an optimistic remaining-cost table. Relax distinct remaining room visits into a fixed number of steps that may repeat places. The easier problem provides a lower bound. It is built once per solve, not per robot action.
  4. Expand the best partial order. Choose lowest accumulated expected cost plus heuristic. For each unvisited room, add survival-weighted cost, update survival and visited set.
  5. Prune dominated states when a competing partial route is provably no worse under the assumptions. This is why cost consistency matters.
  6. Repeat until a complete open order or a resource limit. There is no required return to the start.
  7. Reconstruct and rescore. Follow parent pointers, independently compute returned-order cost, and report order, lower bound, expansions, duration, source and guarantee.

The default is exact RPT*, not focal F-RPT*. A configured focal variant allows bounded suboptimality. The room wrapper uses a 2.0 s wall-time solve budget—not a call frequency and not a bound on LLM or matrix-build time.

Two distinct fallbacks

A budget-exhausted routing search can return a constructive nearest-neighbor tour with no optimality guarantee. Separately, a missing/unusable instance or caught adapter exception falls back to a probability-weighted single-room draw. Both are recorded. A nonempty order is not proof that exact RPT found it.

Read status, guarantee, route source, capped/skipped rooms and timing. “Optimal” means optimal over selected destinations, estimated matrix and supplied probabilities; it does not certify object recognition, full-building completeness, success under 500 actions or best achievable SPL.

Eligibility requires a goal coordinate, relative room weight ≥0.01 and no active deferral. A visited room normally cools for 120 action-clock seconds; if every survivor is cooling, that cooldown is waived. Three unproductive attempts defer a room for 180 action-clock seconds. Mapped, budget-spent and stalled visits are considered productive bookkeeping outcomes, not proof that the target is absent.

3.2 · Optional hierarchy

Bounded FALCON, step by step

This is a ROS-free 2D/2.5D adaptation of coverage-guided exploration, not the original aerial binary and not a renamed default frontier heuristic.

  1. EXPLORE →Local CP/SOP viewpoints.
  2. REASON →Refresh semantic room evidence.
  3. SELECT →RPT chooses a region.
  4. TRANSIT →A* follows a committed route.
  5. EXPLORENew burst after entry.

VERIFY and RECOVER can interrupt. DONE terminates. Up to five zero-motion phase transitions occur inside one policy call, so REASON → SELECT → TRANSIT need not cost idle simulator actions. A transition-guard failure is terminal rather than an infinite loop.

Startup and scope

Begin local exploration without requiring a room label or opening panorama. Anchor a 4 m radius envelope at the observed start. If a room exists there, intersect its mask; otherwise use a provisional region. Later geometry can refine it, but the envelope does not follow the robot indefinitely or chase doorway jitter.

Other observed rooms are excluded from the local scope; a bounded unknown apron supplies guidance only. Split regions inherit overlapping visit history; merged regions inherit multiple overlaps. The budget belongs to a burst token, not a room ID, so relabeling cannot refill it.

Inside one local planning transaction

  1. Maintain frontiers. Update free/unknown boundaries from changed observations; split long clusters along their principal geometric direction.
  2. Find physical reachability. Use observed free space, body-radius clearance and local scope. Executable paths do not cut obstacle corners diagonally.
  3. Sample viewpoints. Ring radii are 0.4, 0.8 and 1.2 m, with 16 angular samples. Check reachable position, frontier visibility and unknown-space gain using camera field of view, depth range, pitch and occlusion—not omnidirectional sensing.
  4. Keep alternatives. Up to four viewpoints per frontier; quantize yaw to the action lattice; remember visited/rejected position-heading pairs. An already-observed zero-motion orientation is not new information.
  5. Decompose geometry. Use 4 m coarse tiles and separate safe-free/unknown connected zones. Rectify representatives into components; build connectivity/portal edges and exclude isolated unknown zones.
  6. Plan coverage (CP). Solve an open asymmetric ordering over active-free viewpoint zones and relevant unknown-zone centers. The first destination must be active free. Unknown paths have penalty 2 and are hypothetical guidance, never execution permission.
  7. Apply sequential ordering (SOP). Preserve precedence of remaining coverage elements while inserting frontiers from the current/next selected zones. Only the viewpoint prefix before the next guidance element is immediately useful; later work is deferred, not marked searched.
  8. Refine viewpoint choices jointly. A layered shortest-path optimization chooses compatible alternatives across the prefix and includes a terminal guidance cost where available.
  9. Build executable routes. Produce actual free-space paths and viewing yaw. Commit the first viewpoint and replan after completion/invalidation, not blindly through an entire hypothetical unknown-space tour.

Ordering uses exact subset dynamic programming up to 10 nodes, then deterministic beam search of width 128. It is not upstream LKH; beam results are not exact. Motion cost approximates serial forward actions and path yaw changes rather than simultaneous aerial translation/rotation.

A transaction has a 1.5 s cooperative deadline, an 18-frontier sampling cap, 18,000 grid-node cap and 64 order-node cap. Native numerical work is bounded and checked before/after, not forcibly preempted at exactly 1.500 s. Failure/capacity/deadline outcomes are recorded; no hidden switch to the frontier baseline occurs.

3.3 · Interrupts

Detection, approach, verification and STOP

Both variants use the same target evidence/approach mechanism. A depth-backed accepted target hypothesis can guide approach before it is fully confirmed. A* handles approach; RPT does not. The standoff accounts for the 0.75 m proximity gate, 0.25 m endpoint tolerance, map resolution and goal snapping; under defaults its lower bound is the 0.18 m physical radius.

  1. Maintain support per associated landmark, once per frame.
  2. Require at least three observations and at least one view separated from first support by 0.20 m translation or 20° yaw.
  3. A gap above eight steps resets support; a selected candidate more than eight steps unseen is forgotten.
  4. Near target/approach endpoint, request at most two verification turns for that hypothesis.
  5. Reject unsupported/stalled candidates, clear their support, and suppress them for 20 steps.
  6. On fresh support and target distance ≤0.75 m, emit STOP. The evaluator independently judges the final position.

The three frames need not all be independently separated. Correlated false predictions can therefore satisfy the gate. “Confirmed landmark” after two ordinary observations, “confirmed target” after stricter support, and GT success are distinct.

FALCON saves the interrupted route and remaining burst token, allocates at most 24 target actions, and resumes after rejection/loss. Route-only watchdog clocks exclude the explicit pause; actual global and active-burst budgets do not. Recovery during verification remains inside the same target allowance. The default does not have this same burst/route-bank orchestration.

4.1 · Ground-truth evaluation

Success is independently measured

The evaluator does not reward a confident explanation. A policy-confirmed target can fail, and a policy can finish successfully at the action cap without STOP. Final judgment comes from the declared protocol.

Build the private FMM field

  1. Choose the episode’s scene/floor/category from the private dataset row.
  2. Dilate semantic free-space channel 0 with a two-cell disk (10 cm at 5 cm resolution).
  3. Dilate the target-category plane with a 20-cell disk (1 m); all acceptable category instances contribute.
  4. Mask nontraversable cells, set the goal dilation to zero and compute a Fast Marching Method distance field. The SemExp reference deliberately unmasks goal cells rather than intersecting them with traversability.
  5. Fill masked/unreachable cells with maximum valid distance plus one cell, preserving the reference finite sentinel.
  6. Map actual Habitat XYZ to the GT array’s row/column using its origin and truncation convention. Multiply cell distances by 0.05 for metres.

This FMM is the scorer, not the planner commanding our robot. Euclidean distance to a detected box center, navmesh distance and grid-A* distance are not interchangeable substitutions.

When the episode ends

The environment ends at STOP or 500 actions. It does not terminate on first GT success-region entry. That first entry is private diagnostic telemetry; the robot may subsequently leave and fail.

Success is final FMM distance equal to zero. The local profile does not additionally require STOP. A 500-action episode can succeed without STOP if its final position qualifies; a confident STOP outside fails.

Exact local rule versus paper prose.

OSG p.11 describes a position within 1 m from which the object can be directly observed. The local SemExp-derived field does not run a separate final RGB visibility/instance test, keeps the start floor’s GT map, and uses horizontal coordinates. These limit exact-equivalence and multi-floor claims. Changing the scorer requires a separately named profile, not silently changing the published comparison.

Diagnostics and fairness

  • Observed area: union of ever-observed occupancy cells, including occupied ones, not GT coverage. Initial view is excluded from gained-area/action; floor resets maintain separate masks and can double-count a revisited floor.
  • Collisions: native flags include partial sliding contacts, unlike the policy’s <0.01 m blocked threshold. Compare rates normalized by movement exposure as well as totals.
  • First success action: private first-region entry, not necessarily verified STOP. Raw actions-to-success reporting refers to first entry for finally successful episodes.
  • Revisits, reversals, stagnation: diagnostic counts, not independent success labels.
  • Latency: separate detector HTTP, model requests, mapping, local planning, room selection and full decisions. Common A* counts exclude FALCON’s local CP/SOP work.

Use identical frozen starts/categories, sensors, legal actions, scorer, detector/LLM identities and seeds. Keep complete batches, not the best episodes across attempts. Include navigation failures and declared agent errors in the denominator; distinguish infrastructure failure. Report per-scene/category results because aggregate SR hides imbalance.

The harness uses Wilson SR intervals, seeded bootstrap intervals for mean SPL, exact McNemar tests for paired success and sign-flip permutation for paired SPL differences. One start per building has wide uncertainty. A future larger multi-start study should consider scene-clustered uncertainty rather than treating all starts as fully independent.

4.2 · The three supplied papers

Methods to learn from, not interchangeable benchmarks

The reviewed PDFs are ApexNav v3, SG-Nav v1 and OSG Navigator v1, already extracted in external paper workspaces. Only OSG Navigator reports Gibson results. Non-Gibson scores are intentionally excluded.

ApexNav: adaptive exploration and revisable target identity

ApexNav addresses weak semantic cues and confidently wrong detections. Its genuine contributions are an adaptive geometry/semantics strategy and target-centric multi-label fusion, built around existing detectors, mapping and path planners.

  1. Prepare category knowledge offline: an LLM proposes similar objects, likely rooms and a confidence threshold.
  2. Map geometry and relevance: depth builds occupancy; BLIP-2 image-text scores project target/room relevance with viewing-confidence weights.
  3. Assess semantic usefulness: maximum-to-mean score ratio and spread determine whether semantic cues are informative.
  4. Select strategy: semantic mode orders high-score frontiers with an asymmetric TSP/LKH formulation; weak-cue geometry mode uses nearest-frontier exploration rather than full coverage.
  5. Fuse target/confuser labels: detect and segment objects, associate spatial clusters, retain alternative labels and combine support using observed point volume. Missing a previously expected visible label can reduce its confidence.
  6. Navigate safely: A* supplies waypoint guidance; discrete action choice balances progress and obstacle safety. Reliable target evidence supersedes exploration.

Our target count/separation gate is simpler: it does not maintain competing labels and visibility-conditioned negative evidence. Our local FALCON is geometric coverage planning, not ApexNav’s adaptive semantic/nearest switch. Relevant ideas are revisable target belief and requiring semantic guidance to justify its cost.

SG-Nav: object/group/room context and re-perception

SG-Nav organizes a scene into objects, related groups and rooms to give the LLM structured context rather than a flat object list.

  1. Associate open-vocabulary instances online from posed RGB-D.
  2. Batch language-model proposals for relations between new and existing nodes; check joint-image short relations with a VLM and long relations with geometric/room constraints.
  3. Construct object-centered subgraphs containing nearby relations and higher-level context.
  4. Use successive semantic questions to estimate target proximity/relevance; combine subgraph evidence at frontiers with distance weighting.
  5. Select a frontier and use FMM for local navigation.
  6. Re-perceive a target from additional views, accumulating credibility from detector confidence and graph context before committing.

Our room prompts do not contain SG-Nav’s full object/group/relation hierarchy; our target confirmation does not use graph-conditioned credibility. The useful mechanisms are richer local target context and explicit re-perception, not simply “ask the LLM more often.”

Evaluation: the supplied paper reports MP3D, HM3D and RoboTHOR, not Gibson. Its “at each step” description does not imply one constant-cost query each action.

OSG Navigator: places, connectors and environment schemas

Open Scene Graphs for Open-World Object-Goal Navigation uses topological-semantic memory organized by an environment schema: objects, places, connectors and optional higher regions.

  1. Choose a schema: home rooms/corridors/floors or another vocabulary. Its separate automatic-schema pipeline describes, canonicalizes and iteratively verifies permissible structure.
  2. Parse images: GroundingDINO detects objects/connectors; BLIP-2 supplies place and appearance descriptions. Store text and object-image crops.
  3. Recognize the current place: match observed semantic object features to similar, nearby existing places, not our perfect metric-pose lookup.
  4. Update memory: associate/add objects and places, infer connections from observation/history, and construct schema-defined abstractions.
  5. Reason coarse to fine: an LLM selects promising regions; graph Dijkstra routes to the selected place.
  6. Ground local movement: choose object/connector crops along the route. Real robots use image-goal ViNT; simulation standardizes an FMM controller.

The general system describes ~10 Hz local control, 2–2.5 Hz visual perception and LLM reasoning taking several seconds after subgoal arrival. These are not our runtime frequencies. OSG’s RGB-only/non-metric high-level framing differs from our metric RGB-D/pose stack.

The paper also proposes a probabilistic map extension with particle-filter topology hypotheses. This is not RPT’s survival model and not an uncertainty tracker implemented in our room graph. The main mapper is described deterministically. Headline simulation uses a hand-designed household schema except the separate schema-generation experiment.

Gibson evidence: Table 2, p.12; 1,000 tiny-validation episodes. NM characterizes its representation; a metric FMM simulation controller remains below that semantic layer.

5.1 · Existing flaws

Mechanism, evidence, and improvement

A demonstrated behavior is not always a demonstrated root cause. The labels below distinguish source-verified issues, direct probes, recorded outcomes and design inferences. None of the proposed changes was implemented during this documentation task.

1. Premature bounded termination Measured

Mechanism: a burst ends; its region is cooling/at its revisit cap; no other region is eligible; SELECT immediately enters DONE. The useful-partial exception applies specifically to action-budget expiry with sufficient gain, not every blockage/low-gain ending. A fixed 4 m scope can also finish while a large room remains partly unseen.

Why flawed: “no region presently eligible” is a scheduling/reachability outcome, not proof the task is impossible. It can permanently fail with most of the global budget left. Mifflinburg, Allensville and Pinesdale show early failures; Newfields shows the longer version.

Improve: distinguish cooldown, temporary map/planning failure, exhausted geometry and global-budget termination. Add bounded reason-specific re-observation/recovery and authorized scope expansion/re-anchoring. Do not fix early failure by making retries unbounded or driving through unknown cells.

2. The probability model is not yet a complete ZSON search model Verified

Mechanism: RPT multiplies independent miss probabilities; the oracle supplies normalized room weights scaled by mapped-presence mass. Those are not calibrated independent chances of detecting an acceptable instance during a finite search.

Why flawed: for one mutually exclusive target location, surviving masses 0.3 and 0.3 leaves 0.4, not the product 0.49. But Gibson can contain multiple acceptable instances, so switching blindly to a single-target formula is not universally correct either. Detection yield also depends on visibility, detector recall and time spent searching.

Improve: first define single instance, any category instance, multiple required instances or possible absence. Model success under bounded visual effort; then derive/test objective, heuristic and pruning together. Changing only the survival recurrence does not automatically preserve correctness guarantees.

3. “Expected time to find” omits search and turn costs Verified wiring

Both active room-matrix calls leave search service time and frontier blending at zero. RPT costs are distance divided by nominal forward speed, while actual room search, turns, collisions and verification consume many actions. An ordering optimal for transit can fail under the action budget.

Improve: estimate entry/turn/search cost and detection yield per bounded region visit; test budget-aware search objectives. Revalidate assumptions for heading/history-dependent costs. Report SPL for comparability but do not call it wall-time or action efficiency.

4. Room costs and executable paths disagree at diagonal corners Direct CPU reproduction

The room-cost graph connects finite diagonal cells without requiring orthogonal side cells to be passable. FALCON’s free-route graph rejects that corner cutting.

Minimal executed check:

In a 2 × 2 grid with only opposite diagonal cells free, the room-cost graph returns √2 cells. FALCON’s route distances report unreachable. This is a synthetic geometry probe, not a Gibson score.

Why flawed: the matrix may underestimate a detour or assert a physically impossible connection. Triangle inequality can still hold on the wrong graph; a mathematical guarantee does not repair physical-model mismatch.

Improve: share the no-corner-cut passability contract between costs and execution; add corner-touch/narrow-portal/swept-footprint regressions. Also bound matrix construction itself: the 12-room solver cap happens after all-room cost work.

5. Repeated detections are not robust target identity Failures recorded; cause mixed

Three frames and one sufficient viewpoint change can confirm a hypothesis. Correlated errors can satisfy this; nearby same-class instances can merge within 0.70 m, while changing labels create separate hypotheses. The map keeps averaged points, not competing 3D identity/label beliefs. Box depth can represent the wrong surface.

Ranchester, Woodbine and Wainscott stop with policy-confirmed targets but fail the evaluator. The records do not uniquely separate detector confusion, association/range, approach and annotation/scorer alignment.

Improve: label these failure causes independently; retain segmentation/spatial support and competing labels; require informative independent views; add visibility-conditioned negative evidence. ApexNav/SG-Nav mechanisms are relevant hypotheses, not guaranteed cures. Measure any VLM verification’s latency and correlated-error risk.

6. Room affinity is over-interpreted as calibrated probability Verified

Affinity anchors, shrinkage, size, decay and noisy-OR are handcrafted. Provisional/low-confidence room labels still enter the affinity prompt as selected types. Six alphabetically sorted unique classes can omit important context; area influences both the prompt and numerical weight. Credited room time can include turning/transit rather than inspection.

Improve: propagate room-type uncertainty, prioritize target-relevant evidence, calibrate search yield on development scenes and discount by visibility-qualified negative evidence. Compare against fixed-prior/no-LLM ablations before attributing improvements to language reasoning.

7. The named half-life is actually an e-folding time Direct CPU reproduction

The effort decay uses exp(−elapsed/scale). At 90 action-clock seconds in a 20 m² room with remaining frontiers, the factor is 0.367879, not 0.5. The effective half-life is about 62.4 seconds.

Why flawed: a researcher tuning a stated 90-second half-life gets different behavior. Improve: rename/document an e-folding scale, or use a logarithm-of-two factor for true half-life semantics. A behavior change requires new configuration identity and measurements; do not retroactively relabel results.

8. Room geometry can drift without the right evidence reset Verified structure

Partition revision tracks room-ID/door-ID sets, so mask shape can change without revision. Conversely, changed sets can reset stability broadly. Strict point-in-free-room-mask assignment can leave obstacle-surface objects unassigned. Labels, effort or visit history may therefore describe a changed region or omit useful objects.

Improve: version meaningful mask changes, transfer evidence by geometric overlap, assign objects with boundary/nearest-region uncertainty, and evaluate room segmentation/classification separately. FALCON overlap history improves budget continuity but is not a probabilistic room-identity model.

9. Completion checks may repeatedly read the same evidence Verified cadence interaction

The default supervisor asks for three zero-frontier updates while room facts normally refresh every ten observations. Three calls can read the same graph measurement. Frontier-count stagnation also does not mean zero useful area or no target-bearing views.

Improve: debounce on fresh graph revisions and combine count with visibility, gain and reachability. Separate observed geometric completion from exhaustive target inspection.

10. Stable commitment can keep stale choices Design trade-off

Ignoring small probability changes during transit prevents oscillation. Yet the default may keep an order suffix despite major probability drift, while FALCON re-solves after each region and executes only the first room. These are different approximations to receding-horizon search.

Improve: retain short-term commitment but reconsider meaningful expected improvement at safe boundaries. Record proposals versus executed room changes and test hysteresis. Calling RPT every frame is not a principled solution.

11. Conservative geometry and recovery can deadlock Blockage measured

Unknown/inflation can eliminate outgoing moves. FALCON adds a stricter action veto; recovery rotates rather than using a new reverse primitive. A failed forward is stamped ahead as obstacle evidence, but no movement alone does not localize an obstacle. Persistent map mistakes can trigger terminal failure.

Improve: separate contact evidence from confident obstacle geometry; use bounded alternative views and retained safe escape paths; test narrow-gap action-lattice feasibility. Never delete real obstacles just to make a desired path possible. Separate the safety-layer ablation from the explorer ablation.

12. Coverage and target search are different objectives Objective-based inference

FALCON CP/SOP organizes coverage, while ObjectNav rewards detecting a target before a limit. Local FALCON is geometric; semantics enter at boundaries, not every viewpoint. A view revealing floor area may offer little target-detection value.

Improve: test target-conditioned information gain and adaptive geometric/semantic exploration. Higher observed m²/action is insufficient if reliability falls.

13. Error handling and counters differ across variants Verified control flow

Frontier propagates room-LLM internal failures; FALCON records them and terminates. Model/service failures can therefore enter aggregate outcomes differently. Final graph counters reset on floor changes, and accepted affinity rounds are not total requests.

Improve: standardize failure taxonomy and experiment policy, record immutable episode-wide call counts/retries/latencies, and distinguish navigation failure from unavailable infrastructure. The campaign’s zero agent errors alone is not a universal accounting guarantee.

14. The benchmark does not establish deployment robustness Known scope

Ideal depth/pose, static reconstructions, no dynamics, six categories and a planar start-floor scorer define a controlled experiment. Success dilation need not prove final visibility. Habitat versions differ. OpenAI-compatible requests also do not necessarily receive every seed/token option used in the Ollama path, despite a nominal shared configuration.

Improve: preserve the reference profile and name separate sensor-noise, multi-floor, action and embodiment profiles. Record actual request settings. Never pool robustness results into the published Gibson table.

15. Sampling does not support a broad superiority claim Measured limit

The latest batch has one start in each of 15 training buildings and no TV episode. Earlier validation starts were inspected. No full current-method 1,000-episode result was established. Freezing ensures reproducibility, not unseen data.

Improve: broaden training-development coverage across categories, distances and headings, predeclare ablations, then freeze and evaluate the entire published validation set with all failures/caveats. Report intervals and per-scene/category behavior.

Sources: C02, C03, C12, C13, C14, C16, C17, C24, C27, C28; recorded evidence E1. The two tiny probes were executed during this audit; no algorithm was modified.

Three levels of extension

  1. Same categories, new starts: reuse existing mapping/aliases/actions/scoring; develop on generated training starts and retain original validation.
  2. New categories/language: extend detector vocabulary and grounding contract and obtain corresponding category/instance annotations. The existing GT planes cannot score “a red chair beside a desk” or an absent fire extinguisher.
  3. New environments/sensing: adapt place/room representation, perception and action interfaces. A fixed room-type list plus floor-reset map is not full open-world, multi-floor navigation.

Recommended staged plan

  1. Repair consistency first: no-corner-cut costs, fresh-evidence debouncing and error accounting, with small regression scenarios.
  2. Fix premature termination: distinguish temporary ineligibility from task failure; retain useful partial search without removing budget/safety guarantees.
  3. Audit target failure causes: recognition, association, approach/range and GT alignment need separate labels.
  4. Define the probability model: category/multi-instance/absence semantics and detection under finite search effort must agree with routing.
  5. Add one semantic mechanism at a time: OSG connectors/schema, SG-Nav contextual re-perception, ApexNav negative multi-label fusion/adaptive exploration.
  6. Run matched training-development ablations: hold starts, detector, LLM and scoring fixed, then test the integrated hierarchy.
  7. Freeze for complete validation: retain exposure disclosures; report new protocols separately.

Do not pass GT rooms/goals into observations to “help” planning, treat an LLM negative as proof of absence, give free panoramas, silently change success radius, tune repeatedly on full validation, or transfer a numerical RPT guarantee to the entire mission.

The end-to-end mental model

See locally → build uncertain geometric/semantic memory → choose where to search → execute safe discrete motion → re-observe → stop for an explicit reason → let an independent GT evaluator judge the final outcome. The hard part is making beliefs, budgets, geometry, evidence and scoring agree throughout the loop.
